## Start the app

Two processes: the mock API and the app.

```bash
pnpm start:api       # json-server on http://localhost:3002 (json-server/db.json)
pnpm nx serve core   # the app on http://localhost:3000
```

Set `VITE_API_URL` (see `apps/core/.env.example`) to point the app at another API.

### The FastAPI core service and sign-in

The Categories, Technologies, Clients, Projects, Countries and Users pages, the Resources pages (People, Allocation and Leave), and sign-in itself, are wired to the real backend, the FastAPI service in `octacore-backend`. Adding, editing and deleting a category, a technology, a client, a project, a country or a user writes to it. A client has one primary contact (name, email and phone), kept in the service's `client_contact` table and saved by the same dialog; deleting a client deletes its contact. A technology can have one category (optional). A project has a project type (resource augmentation or product) and a billing type (retainer or fixed cost), and can name a billing country (None means its client's country): the project form lists the active countries that have a rate, labelled like India · INR, and says what a new invoice would be billed in. The project's own page (click its name) lists its resources, the people on it, each with a category and an hourly rate (a resource can link to a person record, but need not), and its invoices. An invoice is made of lines (a description, a quantity and a rate), and its amount is their sum less the invoice's discount. An invoice is billed in a currency and carries a tax rate, both taken from a country when it is created: the project's billing country, else its client's country (only while that country is active and has a rate), else US dollars with no tax. Rates and amounts are typed in that currency, as units and hundredths (`1500.50`); the invoice form says what it is billed in (like `Billed in INR · ₹88.25 per $1 · tax 18%`), has a Tax rate field that starts from the country's and can be changed for the invoice (0 to 100 with up to two decimals, 0 for none), and shows live totals: the subtotal, the discount (floored), the tax (on the amount after the discount, half up) and the total, plus, for an invoice in another currency, the US dollars the amount before tax comes to at the invoice's rate. The totals match the service to the unit. An invoice keeps the currency, the rate (units per US dollar) and the tax rate it was issued with, whatever a later sync of the rates does; only its tax rate can be changed afterwards. A project whose own country has lost its rate cannot be invoiced until a rate is typed or synced. A resource's rate stays in US dollars; a retainer invoice starts from the resources' rates converted at the country's rate. A new invoice on a retainer project starts with one line per resource at 160 hours each, which you can change before saving; on a fixed cost project it starts with one blank line. A resource can stand for several people (its Qty), and a line is Qty x hours x rate. An invoice has a billing period; its due date fills itself to three months after the issue date and the period end to the end of the start's month. A project can have a key (like TKO) and a billing address: an invoice left without a number is numbered from the key, like OCTA/26-27/TKO/007. Each invoice can be previewed, downloaded as a PDF and emailed from the project's page; the server draws the PDF and sends the email, and needs the company, bank and mail settings described in the backend README. What a project is worth comes from its invoices, in US dollars: each invoice counts as its amount (after the discount, before tax) converted at its own rate, so invoices in different currencies add up. The total value is every invoice, and what is outstanding is the ones not yet marked paid; the Overview, the overdue figure and the largest-outstanding list add up the same dollars. Deleting a project deletes its resources and invoices. The Invoices page, under Finance, lists every project's invoices with their client and project. It shows the ones not yet paid (overdue included) until you change the filters: status, client, project and the issue date range. Each invoice is listed at what its client pays, the total with tax, in its own currency. Export CSV saves the rows shown, with the amount before tax, the tax and the total in the invoice's own currency, and its code in a Currency column. Invoices are still added, edited and marked paid on the project's own page. If the service is down, a page shows an error with a Try again button.

The Expenses page (under Finance) and the Expense types page (under Reference data) are wired to the same service. An expense is one charge: a name, a type, a charge date, an amount, an optional project, an optional person (from People) and notes. A recurring expense (a subscription, a tool, rent) has a frequency (monthly, quarterly or yearly), a start date and an optional end date, and the service creates one expense for every charge date from the start date on, at startup and every 24 hours, each carrying the recurring expense's person; saving one with a start date in the past creates its charges at once and says how many. A recurring expense can be paused (the charges it missed are not created when it resumes), resumed, cancelled, or deleted (its charges stay). Amounts are US dollars, kept as cents. To type an amount in another currency, choose the country it is in (a picker labelled like Germany · EUR): a country carries its currency and its rate, how many of that currency make one US dollar, and US dollars are always 1. The picker offers the active countries that have a rate. The expense then keeps the amount typed and the rate it was converted at, and a later change of the rate never changes it. The Expenses page has three tabs, kept in the address (`?tab=`): **Overview** (the bare address) shows one month, this month by default, with previous and next month buttons (`?month=`): its total split into one-time and recurring, the total by type, and every expense in it, one-time and generated charges together, filtered by type, project and person; **One-time** lists the expenses typed in by hand for the year, filtered by type, project, person, dates and a search, totals what it shows, and exports it as CSV; **Recurring** lists the recurring expenses with what the active ones cost each month and year. The old Recurring link still works: it opens the Recurring tab. `make seed` in the backend adds a starter set of expense types.

The Countries page (under Reference data) is a full list of countries (`make seed` in the backend loads them) that you can add to, edit and delete. A country has a name, ISO, ISO3, numeric and calling codes (the table lists the name and ISO code; the form edits all of them and the search finds a country by any code but the numeric one), its currency (code, name and symbol), a rate (units of that currency per US dollar, optional until typed or synced, and always 1 for a country that uses US dollars), a tax rate (a percentage from 0 to 100, the tax a new invoice in that country starts with) and an active flag. The active countries are the ones offered when you pick a country for a client or an expense; the page can show all of them or only the active ones. **Sync rates** fetches the rates of the active countries (not the US dollar ones) from the online exchange-api and says how many it updated and which currencies had no rate. It runs only when you press it: nothing syncs on a schedule. A country that a client, a project, an expense or a recurring expense uses cannot be deleted, nor can the last active country that uses US dollars be switched off, deleted or given another currency; the dialog shows the service's message. The Currencies page and `/currencies` no longer exist: expenses and recurring expenses use a country instead.

The Resources group (People, Allocation and Leave in the sidebar, at `/resources/*`; `/people` redirects to `/resources/people`) is wired to the same service. **People** are staff: a name, contact, joined and left dates, a contract link, a monthly salary typed in rupees and paise (`85000.00`, shown as `₹85,000.00`) and the technologies they know; the list is searched by name, email, phone or technology, and a name opens the person's page (tabs Overview, Allocations and Leave, in `?tab=`). **Allocation** has a Board tab, the bench first and then a column for each active project with the monthly salary cost of its people, where the board fills the window and a card is dragged from anywhere on it to another column (or moved with the Move to menu at its top right), which saves a move dated today; and a Ledger tab, every dated entry ("from this date the person is on this project, or on the bench"), where entries are added, edited and deleted, so a past date can be backfilled. A person has one entry a date and is on one project at a time. **Leave** is the register of leaves taken (a type, a first and last day, a reason); a person's leaves may not overlap. Deleting a person deletes their allocation entries and leaves too. A retainer project's team margin counts salaries in US dollars at India's rate from the Countries page.

Every page needs a signed-in person. The service keeps the accounts; there is no sign-up, so create the first admin on the backend:

```bash
# in octacore-backend: once, then whenever you need it
make setup     # database, migrations, a JWT_SECRET in .env
ADMIN_EMAIL=you@example.com ADMIN_PASSWORD='at least ten characters' ADMIN_NAME='Your Name' make seed-admin
make run       # the API on http://localhost:5001/api/v1
```

`make seed-admin` creates the admin only if no live user has that email, and never changes an existing password. On a scratch database, put `DB_NAME=<scratch db>` in front of it (and of the `uvicorn` command that serves that database).

Keep the app and the API on the same hostname, `localhost` for both: the app on `http://localhost:3000` (the default), the API on `http://localhost:5001`. The refresh cookie only travels between same-site addresses, so mixing `localhost` and `127.0.0.1` signs you out on every reload. The API allows credentialed calls only from the origins in its `CORS_ORIGINS` setting (by default `http://localhost:4200` and `http://localhost:3000`); if the app runs anywhere else, set `CORS_ORIGINS` on the API to the app's exact origin. Point the app at another API with `VITE_CORE_API_URL` (see `apps/core/.env.example`).

To try sign-in by hand: start the API and the app as above, open `http://localhost:3000/users` and you land on the login page. Sign in with the seeded admin and you come back to the Users page. Reload: you stay signed in (the refresh cookie restores the session). Sign out from the foot of the sidebar: you are back on the login page, and signing in again lands on the overview.

`make run` serves the API on IPv4 only (`127.0.0.1`). Browsers fall back to it when `localhost` resolves to `::1` first, but Cypress's Electron does not. For a Cypress run against the real service, serve the API on `::1` instead, for example `.venv/bin/uvicorn app.main:create_app --factory --host ::1 --port 5001`, and keep `localhost` in `VITE_CORE_API_URL`. The stubbed Cypress specs intercept `http://localhost:5001` and need no service; a request that none of the stubs answers is answered 501 by a catch-all, so a spec cannot reach a real service on that port.

To check the app's expectations against the running service (opt-in, skipped otherwise), sign the checks in with the admin you seeded:

```bash
CORE_API_LIVE=1 CORE_API_URL=http://127.0.0.1:5001/api/v1 \
  CORE_API_TEST_EMAIL=you@example.com CORE_API_TEST_PASSWORD='at least ten characters' \
  pnpm exec nx test core --skip-nx-cache
```

`CORE_API_URL` names the service (default `http://localhost:5001/api/v1`). Signing in writes `last_login_date` and a refresh-token row, so even the read-only checks are not entirely read-only.

The Categories, Technologies, Clients and Users checks add records, edit them and delete them, so they run only when `CORE_API_WRITE_URL` names a service on a scratch database. Seed an admin there and start a second service on it (in `octacore-backend`):

```bash
DB_NAME=<scratch db> ADMIN_EMAIL=smoke@example.com ADMIN_PASSWORD='smoke test passphrase' ADMIN_NAME=Smoke make seed-admin
DB_NAME=<scratch db> .venv/bin/uvicorn app.main:create_app --factory --port 5002
```

then, here:

```bash
CORE_API_WRITE_URL=http://127.0.0.1:5002/api/v1 CORE_API_TEST_EMAIL=smoke@example.com \
  CORE_API_TEST_PASSWORD='smoke test passphrase' pnpm exec nx test core --skip-nx-cache
```

The live checks run in Node and carry the refresh cookie by hand, so the same-site rule above does not apply to them, and `127.0.0.1` reaches the IPv4-only service directly.

The People, Allocation and Leave checks write as well (people, technologies, a project, allocation entries and leaves, all deleted again), so they run only against `CORE_API_WRITE_URL` too. `apps/core-e2e/src/e2e/resources.cy.ts` is the same module in a real browser, against the real service instead of the stubs: it adds a person, allocates them, drags their card to the Bench with real mouse events, backfills the ledger, and adds and deletes a leave. It is skipped unless it is given the service and the admin, it needs a scratch database with no other people on it (the board's cost per column is checked to the paisa), and it opens the page once and then uses the sidebar, because a reload would lose the access token, which lives in memory, and the refresh cookie does not cross from `localhost` to `127.0.0.1`. Start the service with `CORS_ORIGINS=http://localhost:3013` and the app on that port with `VITE_CORE_API_URL` at it, then run Cypress with the same `baseUrl` (and `env -u ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE` when it starts from an editor's terminal):

```bash
env -u ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE pnpm exec cypress run --project apps/core-e2e --config-file cypress.config.ts \
  --config baseUrl=http://localhost:3013 --spec apps/core-e2e/src/e2e/resources.cy.ts \
  --env CORE_API_WRITE_URL=http://127.0.0.1:5013/api/v1,CORE_API_TEST_EMAIL=<email>,CORE_API_TEST_PASSWORD=<password>
```

## Running tasks

To execute tasks with Nx use the following syntax:

```
nx <target> <project> <...options>
```

You can also run multiple targets:

```
nx run-many -t <target1> <target2>
```

..or add `-p` to filter specific projects

```
nx run-many -t <target1> <target2> -p <proj1> <proj2>
```

Targets can be defined in the `package.json` or `projects.json`. Learn more [in the docs](https://nx.dev/core-features/run-tasks).
