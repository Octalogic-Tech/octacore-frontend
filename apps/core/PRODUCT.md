## Platform

web

## Stack

Vite 4.3 and React 19 in the Nx 16.5 workspace `octacore-frontend`. Tailwind 4, shadcn-structured components (Radix for dialogs, and the registry's Kanban on dnd-kit for the allocation board), TanStack Query 5, react-hook-form with zod, react-router 6. Data and sign-in come from the FastAPI core service in `octacore-backend`. A json-server mock (`json-server/db.json`, now empty) is still in the repository, but no page reads it. Versions are recorded in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-14-core-redesign-design.md`, section 15. Hosting is undecided.

## Users

The four Octalogic partners, the company's co-founders. They run the company and look in on money and delivery between client work: what is owed, what needs approving, which projects exist and for whom. They know the data already and want to answer a question and leave, not be guided.

Which devices they use is not confirmed. The app must work on a phone and on a desktop.

## Product Purpose

One place for the partners to do five jobs:

- See what is owed: what is outstanding across projects, and which invoices are unpaid.
- Review invoices: every project's invoices in one list, the unpaid ones by default, filtered by status, client, project or issue date, with a CSV export.
- Look up projects and the clients they belong to.
- See who is on which project, what each team costs a month, who is on the bench and who is away (Resources).
- Keep the reference data (categories, technologies) accurate so everything else stays consistent.

It replaces three separate apps (Core, Projects, Finance) that only linked to each other by port number. Success means the five jobs work end to end in this one app, with no second app or address to remember.

## Operating Context

- Everything is on the real backend, the FastAPI core service in `octacore-backend` (port 5001, base path `/api/v1`, every response in a `{statusCode, message, data, meta}` envelope). It has users, categories, clients, countries, projects, people, technologies, invoices, allocation entries and leaves. Sign-in and the Categories, Technologies, Clients, Projects, People, Allocation, Leave, Invoices, Countries and Users pages use it. A country is added, edited and deleted on the Countries page: it carries its currency (code, name and symbol), a rate (units of that currency per US dollar; always 1 for US dollars; empty until typed or synced), a tax rate (a percentage, 0 to 100) and an active flag, and the active countries are the ones offered wherever a country is picked (a client's country, an expense's country). The Sync rates button on the Countries page fetches the rates of the active countries from the online exchange-api; it runs only when pressed, there is no schedule. A technology has an optional category. A person has an optional monthly salary in Indian rupees (the current figure only: a change overwrites it, and where the person has been is the allocation ledger's business, not the salary's) and the technologies they know; like every other record it is visible to anyone who is signed in. A project has a project type (resource augmentation or product) and a billing type (retainer or fixed cost); it can name a billing country (None means its client's country) and the service says what a new invoice would be billed in (`billing`: currency, rate, tax rate and where it came from); it has resources (people with a category and an hourly rate) and invoices made of lines; every amount is stored as whole minor units (the service sends and takes `rateCents`, `monthlySalaryPaise`, `amountCents` and so on), forms take units and hundredths (`12.50`) and every figure shows two decimals (`$1,234.56`, `₹1,23,456.78`); a resource's rate is in US cents and a person's salary in Indian paise, while an invoice is in the currency of its country: it is issued with that country's currency, its rate (units per US dollar, 1 for dollars) and its tax rate, taken from the project's billing country, else the client's country (only while it is active and has a rate), else US dollars with no tax, and it keeps them (a later sync of the rates never changes it; only its tax rate can be edited); an invoice's rates and amounts are in its currency, its amount is the sum of its lines less its discount (floored), its tax is its tax rate of that amount (half up, once per invoice), its total is the amount plus the tax, and the service also keeps the amount in US cents at the invoice's own rate (`amountUsdCents`, half up, before tax); a retainer invoice starts from the resources at 160 hours each, their rates converted to the invoice's currency at the country's rate, and the project's total value and what is outstanding are calculated in US dollars from its invoices' dollar amounts (before tax), each invoice marked paid when the client pays; the invoice form's live totals (subtotal, discount, tax, total and, for another currency, the dollars) are worked out on integers and match the service to the unit. Each invoice can be previewed, downloaded as a PDF and emailed; a project's key numbers its invoices. An invoice has no status of its own: it is paid once it has a paid date, overdue when unpaid and past its due date, and unpaid otherwise. On the Invoices page "active" means every invoice not yet paid, overdue ones included; its date filters work on the issue date; each invoice is listed at what its client pays, the total with tax, in its own currency.
- Resources is built: one sidebar group (People, Allocation, Leave) at `/resources/people`, `/resources/allocation` and `/resources/leave`; the old `/people` address redirects to the first. Here a resource is a member of staff, not the billing line on a project's Resources tab, which stays where it is and is not linked to allocations. **People** holds the staff: contact, joined and left dates, a contract link, a monthly salary typed and shown in rupees and paise (`₹85,000.00`, Indian digit grouping; whole INR paise on the wire as `monthlySalaryPaise`, and a client still sending `monthlySalaryCents` is refused with a 422 rather than have dollars stored as rupees) and the technologies they know (a plain set picked from the Technologies list, no level). The list is searched by name, email, phone or technology, and a name opens the person's page (`/resources/people/:id`, tabs Overview, Allocations and Leave in `?tab=`): where they are today with an Allocate button, their contact and pay, their own ledger and their own leave. **Allocation** (tabs Board and Ledger) keeps where everyone is as a ledger of dated entries, each saying "from this date the person is on this project, or on the bench"; the latest entry on or before a day says where they are that day, and a person has one entry a date (a second is refused with a message to edit the first). Entries are added, edited and deleted, so a past date can be backfilled without moving anyone today, and a future one moves nobody yet. A person is on one project at a time: splitting them across projects is not modelled. The **Board** shows today: the bench first, then a column for each active project by name, each head giving the head count and the monthly cost in rupees (the sum of its people's salaries; a person with no salary counts as 0 and the column says how many), and a card for each person who has joined and not left. Someone whose latest entry names a project that is no longer active (on hold, completed or deleted) is on the bench with a note naming it. The board is the registry's Kanban component (dnd-kit): a card is dragged by its handle to another column, or moved with the Move to select on the card, which is the way to do it without dragging; both save a move dated today (the browser's local day) that replaces any entry already dated today, a drop on the card's own column saves nothing, and a failed save puts the card back and says so. **Leave** is the register of leaves taken: a type (casual, sick, earned, unpaid or other), a first and a last day (both count; one day has the same date in both) and an optional reason, added, edited and deleted on the Leave page or on the person's page. A person's leaves may not overlap (refused, naming the days that clash); there is no approval, no count of days and no effect on the board. A Google Form or a custom form feeding the register is not built. Deleting a person also deletes their technology links, allocation entries and leaves. A retainer project's team margin converts its people's salaries from rupees to US cents at India's rate on the Countries page (the lowest-id active country with currency INR and a rate), and says so when there is none.
- Expenses are built: one Expenses page under Finance with three tabs (Overview, One-time, Recurring; the tab is in the address), and Expense types under Reference data. An expense is one charge in US dollars (stored as cents) with a type, a charge date, an optional project and an optional person from People. The Overview shows one month at a time (this month first; previous and next month buttons): its total split into one-time and recurring, the total by type, and every expense in it, with filters for type, project and person. One-time is the expenses typed in by hand, with filters, a search and a CSV export. Recurring is the recurring expenses: monthly, quarterly or yearly, from a start date to an optional end date, for which the core service creates an expense for each charge date (at startup and every 24 hours; a start date in the past creates its charges at once), each carrying the recurring expense's person; they can be paused, resumed, cancelled or deleted, and the charges are ordinary expenses that can be edited one by one. An amount can be typed in another currency by choosing the country it is in (the picker reads like Germany · EUR and offers the active countries that have a rate): the country carries the currency and its rate (units per US dollar, US dollars fixed at 1), and an expense keeps the amount typed and the rate it was converted at. The Currencies page is gone; a country replaced it. Open decisions: profit against invoices, a by-person breakdown, and charts.
- The service's country list comes from a legacy dump (`make seed`): it stores 0 for a missing numeric or calling code and an empty string for a missing ISO3 code (the legacy text NULL, which the app also reads as empty). The Countries table lists the name, ISO code, currency, rate, tax rate and active flag; the codes are edited in the form, and the search finds a country by name, ISO, ISO3, calling code or currency.
- The Invoices page keeps the CSV export the old Finance app had for bills; it exports the rows shown, with the amount before tax, the tax and the total in each invoice's own currency, and a Currency column.
- The old Projects and Finance apps are retired; their working parts live here.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Thirteen areas: Overview, Clients, Projects, People, Allocation and Leave (the Resources group), Invoices, Expenses, Categories, Technologies, Countries (editable, from the core service), Expense types, and Users (admins only). The old navigation's Documents, Supports and Setting were dead links and are not part of the app.
- Sign-in is built. Every page sits behind an email and password checked by the core service (it issues the tokens; there is no third-party provider), and the sidebar shows who is signed in with Change password and Sign out. One role exists, `admin`, with full access; admins manage accounts on the Users page.
- Open decisions: hosting; whether to remove the json-server mock now that no page reads it; the GST "Tax Invoice" layout for Indian invoices (GSTIN, HSN/SAC, place of supply) and a tax name, which an invoice does not carry yet; currencies whose minor unit is not hundredths (every currency is treated as having two decimals); which roles beyond `admin` will exist; splitting a person across projects (an allocation is one project at a time, and a split would turn entries into spans with a share); feeding the leave register from a form; viewing the board as of another date (the ledger answers history); leave balances and approval.
- Terminology: the page is "Invoices" (it was "Receipts and bills"); an invoice is paid, overdue or unpaid, and "active" means not yet paid. "Resources" in the sidebar is staff; a project's Resources tab is its billing lines.

## Product Principles

1. **One app, no hand-offs.** Everything a partner does is reachable from one sidebar.
2. **Answers before controls.** The Overview states the numbers in a sentence; tables and forms come after.
3. **Honest about its data.** Loading, empty and failed states say what is true. A missing API is an error with a retry, never a blank panel.
4. **Careful with money.** Destructive actions ask with explicit wording; an exported file is safe to open in a spreadsheet.
5. **Plain and quick.** Short labels and no onboarding or marketing language; the partners know the business.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: semantic landmarks, visible focus, text contrast of 4.5:1 or better, a label on every field, 44px interactive targets, keyboard-operable dialogs and menus, and reduced motion respected.
